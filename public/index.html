The story

How it started, and where it is going

RiskSight began as a focused exploration of how operational data could be used to identify meaningful risk signals and prioritize attention.

Each iteration started from a practical question that the previous version could not answer, and widened the scope: from scoring individual segments, to explaining why something is flagged, to comparing datasets, to understanding unfamiliar data on arrival.

The problem

The problem

Large operational datasets can contain significant signals, but raw data and conventional reporting do not automatically make risk:

Visible

The meaningful signal is buried among thousands of rows and dozens of columns.

Explainable

A number without its evidence is hard to trust and hard to challenge.

Comparable

Without a consistent baseline, it is difficult to say whether things got better or worse.

Prioritized

When everything is reported, nothing stands out. Attention is the scarce resource.

Actionable

Insight only matters if it tells someone what to look at next.

The approach

The approach

RiskSight treats risk as a chain of evidence rather than a single score. Each stage hands something inspectable to the next.

  1. Raw data

    Spreadsheets and exports, exactly as they arrive.

  2. Data discovery

    Detect structure, map fields with a confidence score and hold back anything ambiguous instead of guessing.

  3. Signals

    Statistically unusual values, behaviours and shifts are surfaced as candidates, not conclusions.

  4. Risk evidence

    Each signal is tied to the data that produced it, separating primary evidence from supporting context.

  5. Exposure

    How much is actually at stake, and where is it concentrated?

  6. Prioritization

    Severity and exposure are combined so the most material items rise to the top.

  7. Investigation

    A structured workspace to explore why something is flagged and record the outcome.

  8. Decision support

    Clear summaries and recommended next steps. The decision stays with a person.

What exists today

Current capabilities

Everything on this page is marked as implemented only if it exists and runs in the current build.

Risk Intelligence

Scores every data segment, groups results into severity levels and lists the evidence factors behind each score. A risk workspace aggregates findings by cluster, category and pattern.

Entity Intelligence

An entity-level profile (agents, in the demo data): current risk, an estimated projection (not backtested), likely root cause, similar entities and a recommended next step, laid out as a decision workflow.

Pattern Intelligence

Surfaces hidden patterns, early-warning signals and behavioural similarity between entities. Signals that are not backtested are labelled as indicative.

Investigation

A structured workspace: data readiness, risk summary, "why this is flagged" split into primary and supporting evidence, analyst notes and previous investigations.

Comparison

Puts two datasets side by side and quantifies the change in risk metrics, business metrics and mix.

Data Intelligence

Imports spreadsheets, discovers the schema, maps fields with confidence, and reports readiness before any analysis. Ambiguous fields are held back, not guessed.

Management Intelligence

Executive summary, severity × exposure matrix, priority distribution, peer-relative anomalies and an explicit view of data coverage and limitations.

Statistical Intelligence

Evidence-only statistics: concentration, distributions, outliers, relationships and estimated excess exposure, each with its basis and sample size. Never presented as a risk verdict.

Data Quality Intelligence

Flags mapping ambiguity, excluded rows, outliers and import issues so that data problems are visible instead of silently distorting results.

Principles

Why it is interesting

Evidence over assumptions

Risk assessments should be supported by evidence. Every flag shows the data it came from.

Prioritization over information overload

The system should help identify what matters first.

Explainability

A risk signal should be understandable by the person who has to act on it.

Comparison

Risk becomes more useful when changes can be measured.

Decision support

Analysis should help people decide what to investigate next, not decide for them.

Iterative product development

RiskSight continuously evolves based on new requirements, validation and discoveries.

Built to be checked

The core scoring logic is protected by integrity hashes, and changes are re-verified by an automated regression suite before they are accepted. Where a result is not yet validated, the interface says so.

Status

Current, evolving and future

Three states, kept deliberately separate. Roadmap items are never presented as implemented.

Current

Implemented and working in the present build.

  • Risk scoring with evidence factors and severity levels
  • Entity-level intelligence and decision workflow
  • Pattern, early-warning and similarity detection (indicative)
  • Investigation workspace with analyst notes
  • Side-by-side dataset comparison
  • Schema discovery, field mapping and data-quality grading
  • Management summaries and statistical intelligence
  • Offline, in-browser operation

Evolving

Working, and under active development or validation.

  • Projection and what-if simulation (estimated, not yet backtested)
  • Rule-based assistant for questions about the loaded data
  • Understanding unfamiliar datasets (universal import)
  • Reading guides and clearer statistical explanations
  • Reliability of imports on large and irregular exports

Future / exploration

Concepts being evaluated. Not built.

  • Backtesting against a longitudinal archive of real periods
  • Control charts across many sequential periods
  • Scheduled, repeatable data intake and comparison
  • AI-assisted analyst workflows, such as drafting evidence summaries for human review
